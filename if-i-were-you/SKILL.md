# If I were you

Busy brains get pulled into details: the perfect font, the fifth rewrite, the shiny side project. Meanwhile the things that actually matter this year wait. This skill keeps the big picture in view and asks: if Claude were the user, what would it do next, and what would it leave alone?

The horizon is about a year. The steps are small and doable now. A big picture without a next step is just a vision board.

## Step 1: Know the big picture

First check what you already know: memory, project files, past chats you can search, anything shared in this chat.

You need:
- **who they are**: what they do, what they're juggling,
- **where they want to be in about a year**: 1-3 goals,
- **what's in the way**, and any hard deadlines.

**If you don't have this, ask before anything else.** One short message: they can upload or paste something that explains it (a CV, a goals note, a plan), or answer:
1. What do you do, in a sentence or two?
2. Where do you want to be in about a year? (1-3 things)
3. What's in the way right now?
4. Any hard deadlines coming up?

Then stop and wait. Don't guess someone's life goals.

When they answer, offer once to keep it ("Want me to remember this, so you don't have to explain it again?"). If yes, save it with today's date in memory if you have it; otherwise suggest they paste it into their preferences or project instructions.

**If the big picture you have is more than about 3 months old,** start with one line: "Last time your goals were X and Y. Still true?" Update it if they say no.

## Step 2: Answer in three parts

**1. The big picture, in one line.**
"You're trying to: <goal>, <goal>."

**2. What I'd do next.** 1-3 steps that move a goal forward, real deadlines first. At least one takes 20 minutes or less, so there's always a way to start. Concrete: "Send Anna the draft", not "work on networking". Each step gets one line on which goal it serves.

**3. Not worth it right now.** The details they're spending energy on that don't move any goal: polishing, side projects, rabbit holes you can see in the chat or in memory. Name them kindly but plainly, with one line why. If there's nothing to call out, skip this part. Don't invent problems.

End with one line on what you based this on, so they can correct you.

## Mid-task check

When they ask "is this worth my time?" (or you see them deep in a detail and they ask for your take): answer **yes, not now, or no**, with one line tying it to their big goals. If it's "not now", say what to do instead. Keep it to two or three lines.

## Rules

- Never invent facts about the user, their goals, deadlines or other people. If you don't know, ask or say you're guessing.
- Their goals, not yours. Don't push goals they didn't name, and don't judge the goals themselves unless asked.
- Don't revive things they've parked, and don't add new projects.
- These are suggestions. They decide.
- Informal, candid, short enough to read on a phone. No motivational speeches.
